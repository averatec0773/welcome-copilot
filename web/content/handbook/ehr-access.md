Your Electronic Health Record (EHR) account is the only system where client
information may be created, viewed, or stored. This page covers how to
request access, why a supervisor has to sign off, and what to do if you get
locked out.

## Requesting access

Submit the EHR access request form linked from the IT Hub (Home > Forms >
EHR Access). The form asks for your role, your assigned regional pod, and
the states where you'll be seeing clients. IT provisions the account within
one business day of submission, but you cannot log in with client-facing
permissions until your supervisor countersigns the request — new hires
typically complete this step on day 2, per the first-week checklist.

## Supervisor countersign

Every EHR account requires a supervisor countersignature before it's
active. This isn't a formality — it's how Mentella satisfies the HIPAA
requirement that access to protected health information be authorized by a
named, accountable person. Your supervisor gets an automatic notification
when you submit the form; if 2 business days pass with no countersignature,
ping them directly or escalate to it@mentella.example.

## Password resets

EHR passwords expire every 90 days. Use the self-service reset link on the
EHR login page — you don't need to file a ticket for a routine reset
password request. The self-service flow emails a one-time code to your
@mentella.example address; codes expire after 15 minutes. If you've
forgotten your username, that's also on the EHR login page under "Forgot
username."

## Locked accounts

After 5 failed login attempts, the EHR locks your account for 30 minutes
automatically. If you need it unlocked sooner, or the lock doesn't clear
after 30 minutes, contact IT — self-service reset doesn't work on a locked
account, only a fresh password does. Repeated lockouts in a short window
may trigger a routine security review; this isn't punitive, it's automatic.

## Who to contact

For access requests, password issues, or locked accounts, write to
it@mentella.example with your full name and regional pod. Include a
screenshot of any error message — it speeds up triage. For questions about
what you're allowed to do once you have access, see the HIPAA Do's and
Don'ts doc.
