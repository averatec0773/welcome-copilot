You don't submit claims yourself, but how and when you document a session
directly determines whether it gets paid. This page covers the basics every
clinician needs.

## How sessions are billed

Billing runs off your EHR session note, not off Calendly. Once you sign a
note, the billing team submits the corresponding claim (insurance) or
invoice (self-pay) within 1 business day. Unsigned notes don't generate a
claim at all — the session simply doesn't get billed until you sign it, so
a backlog of open notes becomes a backlog of unpaid claims fast.

## Insurance vs self-pay

Check the client's payer field in the EHR before every session — it's set
at intake but can change if a client's coverage changes. Insurance sessions
require the correct CPT code and a matching authorization on file; self-pay
sessions skip both but still need a signed note for the invoice to go out.
If a client's insurance status looks wrong or missing, flag it to
billing@mentella.example before the session if you can.

## Session notes deadline (48h, billing depends on it)

Every session note is due within 48 hours of the session — this is a hard
deadline, not a guideline, because billing cannot submit a claim on an
unsigned note and insurers have their own filing windows Mentella has to
meet. Notes signed after 48 hours still get billed, but late notes are the
top cause of denied and delayed claims, so treat the window seriously.

## Common claim rejections

The most frequent rejections billing sees: a session note signed after the
payer's filing deadline, a CPT code that doesn't match the documented
service, a missing or expired prior authorization, and a client eligibility
lapse that wasn't caught before the session. Most are preventable by
checking the EHR payer field before you see a client and signing your note
inside the 48-hour window.

## Questions (billing@mentella.example)

For anything about a specific claim, authorization, or rejection, email
billing@mentella.example with the client's EHR record number — never
include diagnosis or session content in the email, only the identifiers
billing needs to look up the claim. General billing policy questions are
welcome there too.
