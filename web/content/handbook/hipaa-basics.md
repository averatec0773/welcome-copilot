HIPAA governs everything you do with client information at Mentella. This
page is a practical guide, not the full policy — when in doubt, ask before
you act.

## What counts as PHI

Protected Health Information (PHI) is any information that could identify
a client combined with anything about their health, treatment, or payment
for care. That includes obvious things like a diagnosis or session note,
but also a name plus an appointment time, a client's initials in a message
to a coworker, or a phone number tied to a case. If you're unsure whether
something counts, treat it as PHI.

## Where PHI may live (EHR only — never email, never sheets)

PHI may only be created, stored, or discussed inside the EHR. It never
belongs in email, Slack, a shared spreadsheet, a personal notes app, or a
text message — even to a supervisor, even about scheduling. If you need to
flag something about a client to a colleague, do it inside the EHR's
internal messaging, not any other channel.

## Devices and screens

Only access the EHR from a company-issued device or an approved secure
connection — never a personal phone or a shared computer. Lock your screen
every time you step away, even for a minute, and position your monitor so
it isn't visible to anyone else in the room during a session or
note-writing. Screenshots of anything in the EHR are not permitted under
any circumstance.

## Incident reporting (report within 24h, no blame for reporting)

If you send PHI somewhere it shouldn't be, misplace a device, or suspect
any other exposure, report it to your supervisor and compliance within 24
hours. Mentella's policy is no-blame for reporting — the goal is
containing the incident fast, not assigning fault. Delayed or unreported
incidents are treated far more seriously than the original mistake.

## This assistant (policies only, never client data)

This handbook assistant can answer questions about Mentella's policies —
like the ones on this page — but it is not connected to the EHR and should
never be asked about a specific client, session, or case. If you catch
yourself typing a client's name or details into it, stop and use the EHR
instead.
