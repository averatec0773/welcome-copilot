Mentella uses Calendly, synced to your Mentella Google Calendar, to manage
every client-facing appointment. This page covers setup, changes, and the
policies that keep scheduling fair to clients across time zones.

## Connecting your calendar

Connect your Calendly account to your Mentella Google Calendar during your
first week (day 3 on the first-week checklist). Use your @mentella.example
address for both — syncing Calendly to a personal calendar isn't supported
and will cause double-bookings. Your intake coordinator can't assign your
first clients until Calendly shows open availability, so don't skip this
step.

## Setting working hours

Set your working hours inside Calendly's Availability tab, not just on your
Google Calendar — Calendly is the source of truth clients book against.
Most clinicians hold 20-25 client-facing hours per week plus buffer time
for notes; block that buffer explicitly so clients can't book into it.
Update your hours at least 1 week before a change takes effect, since
clients may already hold appointments in the old window.

## Rescheduling and cancellations

Clients can reschedule or cancel through their Calendly confirmation email
up to 24 hours before a session with no charge. Inside 24 hours, it's
treated as a late cancellation and billed at the practice's standard
late-cancellation rate. If you need to reschedule a client yourself, give
at least 48 hours' notice when possible and loop in your intake coordinator
for anything inside that window.

## No-show policy

A client who doesn't show and doesn't cancel is a no-show. Document it in
the EHR within the same 48-hour session-note window as any other session,
and flag the appointment as a no-show in Calendly. A client's second
no-show in a rolling 90-day period triggers an automatic check-in from your
pod lead — it's a clinical safety step, not a billing escalation.

## Time zones for multi-state care

Every Calendly event listing shows your time zone, but always confirm the
client's time zone verbally at intake — Mentella serves clients across
multiple states, and Calendly infers time zone from the browser, which is
wrong more often than you'd expect. Set your own time zone correctly in
both Calendly and Google Calendar; a mismatch is the single most common
cause of scheduling errors we see.
